     due to linearization and simpliciations.
b) In the second lab assignment you used LQR in combination with integral effect. In
     this setup, describe how you can tune the strength of the integral effect in the
     controller.
     To introduce integral effect, the system is augmented with two additional inte-
     grator states. LQR is then applied to the augmented system, meaning that the
     optimization is calculated with penalties for deviations from 0 in the integrator
     states as well as in the system’s original state values. If a diagonal Q matrix is
     used, the coefficients for the two integrator states determine how strongly these
     are penalized. High values for these coefficients will imply a higher cost for hav-
     ing integrator values deviating from 0, and will therefore make the controller act
     more strongly to reduce integrator levels, implying stronger integration terms in
     the controller.
c) In the third lab assignment you had to disregard the accurate encoder measurements
     and control the helicopter based on accelerometer and gyroscope measurements from
     an IMU. Why is a state estimator an important tool for controlling the helicopter
     in this case?
     The main reason a state estimator is important here is that the IMU measurements
     are noisy, so controlling the helicopter using the measurements directly for state
     feedback does not work well. A well tuned state estimator gives significantly less
     noisy state estimates, allowing better control.
     State estimators also allow estimation of states that cannot be directly measured.
d) In the fourth lab assignment a discrete time Kalman filter was used to estimate states.
     For the Kalman filter, you may have used a diagonal Qd matrix. When the matrix
     has this structure, what is the effect of increasing or decreasing one of the elements
     along the diagonal?

                                            11

Each element along the diagonal of Qd represents the variance of the (discrete
time white noise) disturbance affecting one particular state value. Increasing one
diagonal element means that we assume a higher variance of that disturbance
signal. This makes the estimated variance (in P) of that state higher, which will
lead to the Kalman filter weighting the a priori estimate of that state less compared
to measurements. Measurements will therefore affect that state more strongly, and
measurement noise will will be suppressed to a lower degree for that state.
